# Voice: Farza's launch playbook

Distilled from Farza Majeed (founder of HeyClicky, previously buildspace): his 47-minute YC Startup School interview on launch videos (23 Oct 2026), his Clicky launch videos, posts and essays. Timestamps point into the interview. Sources are at the end.

## The stance

- **Demos, not launches (2:56).** Every feature is a small launch. Post demos often; save a polished launch for about once a quarter. Ask "what are we launching this week?" and work backwards from the demo.
- **Show the work (25:56).** Show what was built and how, never "a day in my life as a founder".
- **Measure signups, not views (7:10).** The Clicky launch got nearly 3M views, about 7,000 downloads, and about 100 daily users five days later. Those 100 people are the signal.
- **Post and walk away (16:55).** Zero views is still a launch. Check results a week later.
- **Be cringe first (28:11).** The reps make the craft. Ten bad demos come before the good one.

## The formula (21:53)

Every video, every time:

1. This is who I am.
2. This is what's going on.
3. This is what I'm working on.
4. Let me show you.

Setup is 10 to 20 percent of the video (11:15). In a one-minute demo the first 10 seconds say what it is and why it matters, then the demo starts.

## Writing the lines

- **Script every word, then rehearse it until it sounds unscripted (14:00).** His test for a script: would you enjoy reading it if someone texted it to you?
- **Name it plainly in the first sentence.** "Hey everyone, so this is Clicky. It's a little blue guy that follows your cursor."
- **"So check this out"** hands over to the demo. The tone is showing a co-founder something on your laptop (15:37).
- **The problem is yours alone (13:41).** One line of the founder's own pain, often their own laziness. Talk to the one other person who might think it's cool, never to "everyone".
- **Ask the product what a real user would ask.** Naive questions that escalate. Narrate in plain words: "it's pointing me here", "just like that".
- **Keep one flaw in.** Name a wrong answer or a slow step, shrug, move on.
- **Big claim, mundane demo.** The product can be "the simplest interface in the world" while the demo cleans up a desktop.
- **Short lines, one thought each.** Sentence case, casual abbreviations allowed (ppl, tbh, lol, "+" for and). Speech runs looser: like, literally, so cool.
- **End soft.** "Link below, give it a shot." Then a three or four word tagline.

Out: corporate verbs (leverage, empower, revolutionize), feature lists, benchmarks, fake vision ("being fake Steve Jobs ain't it").

## Making it land

- **The "did my eyes stop" test (8:43).** Paste the post text over a random post in the feed and ask whether you would stop. Choose the first frame on purpose: most people see three seconds. Zoom in on the small thing that moves.
- **Beats, not cuts (37:07).** A video can have zero cuts. Each beat moves the story so the viewer keeps asking "where is this going?"
- **Call out the niche in line one (30:34).** "Are you the founder of a design agency? Stop right there." About 4,000 views each, about 50 leads.
- **Watch it muted.** The visuals and captions carry the story without sound.
- **Audio beats camera (41:48).** A cheap clip-on mic; never the laptop or earbud mic. Screen demos in a screen recorder that animates the cursor.
- **Channels convert differently (31:02).** For him: X has the worst conversion, a 20,000-view YouTube video converted at about 20%, and Instagram brings about 70% of traffic. One tracked link per channel.
- **Ship-log videos (24:53).** Every two weeks, "everything we built this week": each person shows their own piece, no hook. Some pass a million views.
- **Study reels as templates (22:15).** "Two months ago I…", "features we should have shipped six months ago". Self-deprecating mini stories.

## Shape of a one-minute demo

| Time      | Beat           | What happens                                                           |
| --------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0:00–0:08 | Name it        | Who I am, the product in one plain sentence                            |
| 0:08–0:20 | The pain       | "So check this out", one line of the founder's own pain                |
| 0:20–1:15 | Live demo      | Two to four real requests, each harder than the last, narrated plainly |
| ~1:15     | The flaw       | One honest wrong answer or slow step                                   |
| ~1:25     | Zoom out       | "It can do X to Y" in one line                                         |
| last 5s   | Call to action | "Link below", then the tagline                                         |

## Post format

Short lines, one thought each:

```
I built this thing called <name>.

<What it is, one line.>

<What it does, one concrete example.>

<One personal verdict.>

Link below.
```

The day before: "Something new. See you tomorrow, link below."
